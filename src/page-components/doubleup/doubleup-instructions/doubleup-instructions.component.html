How do you play this game?

In a Blackjack Variant you lose EV with one rule but then gain it back with some new, juicy twist.

But is it a fair trade?

In Double Up Blackjack, it definately is!

The juicy twist is that, after your first 2 cards, along with the usual play options, you have the additional option to Double Up.

This means you can make a seperate double up bet, up to the size of your original bet, and you don't even take another card.

This second, double up bet only wins if the player's hand beats the dealer's hand. A push looses.

For the original bet, though, a push is still a push.

Then there's the negative EV rule...

If the dealer hits to 16, all hands push.

This includes a push on the double up bet as well.

But, and this adds EV back on the juicy side, hands that hit or double down to 21 do win.

You might equate the push on 16 rule to the pushing on 22 rule that other blackjack variants have.

To be clear, that is a very bad rule, but here's a little food for thought on why the push on 16 rule is not the same EV killer.

Pushing on a 22 is a push on a hand that 100% would have been a win in classic blackjack, but pushing on 16 is a push that would only have most likely been a win.

Also, since hands that hit to 21 do get paid, including double downs, those hands win 100% of the time. In classic Blackjack, they would push 1 in 13 times.

Those things add up.

Another little EV gem is that doubling up is allowed after splitting. So, aces turn into 21s and small fortunes.

But, don't forget, a push is a loss on the double up bet, and it can be a heart breaker.

Just a hint on what to consider that may or may not make it worth it to switch from Hi Lo...

In regular blackjack the 5 is the dealer's best friend since it will never bust the dealer. Well, since a 16 is a push here, the 6 becomes the dealer's new best friend.

Now, couple that with the fact that the 6 has a second advantage to the house with it's tendency to turn what in classic blackjack is a positive EV 16, into a 0 EV hand.
